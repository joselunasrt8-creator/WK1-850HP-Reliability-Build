# Source Record — Trackhawk 6.2 / 8HP95 Transplant Feasibility Research

**Original source:** `Trackhawk 6.2/8HP95 Transplant into 2008 WK1 SRT8: Feasibility & Plan`  
**Role:** earlier Route B feasibility research retained for historical traceability.

## What this source contributed

- Identified Sound German Automotive as a plausible 8HP control/integration path.
- Framed the mechanical swap around a complete Trackhawk 6.2, 8HP95, transfer case, harness/modules, cooling, and supporting donor hardware.
- Estimated custom work for mounts/crossmember, driveshafts, exhaust, cooling, fuel, tuning, and wiring.
- Established that a complete donor has value because it supplies matched mating interfaces and electronic components.

## Historical cost model

The earlier research estimated approximately:

- parts/integration excluding donor: roughly **$4.5k–$8.5k**,
- labor/design/fabrication: roughly **$3k–$6k**,
- likely total excluding donor: roughly **$10k–$11k**, with a broad high estimate near **$14.5k**.

These figures are retained only as historical planning estimates. Later feasibility and Build Book work supersede them where exact scope expanded.

## Important superseded claim

The earlier research was optimistic about bridging the Trackhawk ECU/TCM/BCM network into the WK1 so gauges, ABS, and traction would continue to operate. Later work tightened this substantially:

- Sound German evidence is strong for 8HP transmission integration.
- Public evidence did **not** prove complete MP3015C/DTCM + WK1 ABS/traction integration.
- The Route B Build Book therefore adopted a no-CAN-assumption rule and mandatory bench validation of the donor powertrain/security/AWD network.

Accordingly, this source is useful for lead generation and early architecture history, but later source records control any statement about MP3015C, DTCM, ABS/ESC, or complete Trackhawk network compatibility.
